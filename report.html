Checks behind the choices

  • Only 6.7% of flats have 2+ car spaces, so the yes/no car_space carries what the hand-in needs.
  • 0.0% of attrs_last_seen dates and 0.0% of first_ever_listed dates fall after 31 July 2026.
  • 0 past adverts dated after 31 July and 2 sales after 31 August were dropped.
  • The floor can be read from the unit number for 48% of Task 5 rows; bedrooms are blank for 73%.
  • 6,742 Task 5 rows are shops or offices: valued (no blanks) but kept out of training and our test, as the judges do.

Method

  • One pipeline for both tasks. For each monthly snapshot date we compute every feature only from events before that date, then learn to predict the prices that appear in the next two months, the same horizon the judges test.
  • Prices moved through time with a repeat-pairs index (same flat, two prices; smoothed monthly), rebuilt at every snapshot from earlier pairs only.
  • Anchor plus correction. Each flat starts from its best single guess: its own For Sale advert before the task date, else its own last price moved by the index, else the median of the same building and bedroom count, else the suburb and bedroom median. LightGBM learns the correction from flat size, floor, building, block and neighbouring buildings.
  • Loss close to the median (Huber on log price), because both scores reward typical error, not average error.
  • Parking: the model sees the advert's parking and must price a car space at zero or more (monotone constraint). Each flat is predicted twice, with and without.
  • Our own check: fit before the split date, score on later events up to the task date, never touching them while building.

Parking

Every flat gets two answers; the gap is the parking premium (median $76/week). A monotone constraint stops the model ever pricing a car space below zero.

Does the car-space value hold up in the raw adverts?

Like for like: in the same building, with the same bedrooms, adverts with a car space against adverts without, all moved to July 2026 rents, from the three years before the cut-off. Across 816 such building-and-bedroom pairs (16,152 adverts) a car space added a median $71 a week; our hand-in says $72 for the same flats. In 16% of pairs the flats with parking advertised for less, a reminder that single buildings are noisy.

Why 227 sales is a noisy test
